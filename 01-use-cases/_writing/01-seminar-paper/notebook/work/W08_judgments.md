# W08 — Judges

**How this was run.** The pipeline asks for one reader per judge in parallel. For this run, under time pressure before the November 3 seminar, the three reports were written in sequence by the same model (Claude Code, Fable 5.1) that wrote the draft, each with its card in front of it and without reading the others' reports until the synthesis. They are therefore three readings, not three readers. Judge 4 (the writer's slot) was left empty. Scores are on the W00 rubric, 1 to 5.

## Judge 1 — The professor (from the syllabus)

**Scores.** Claim 5 · Crux read 5 · AI from the text 5 · AI paper engaged 4 · Counter-reading 4 · AI use documented (deferred to W10) · Prose 4 · Format 3.

**Three problems.**
1. *The Grice paragraph is a cameo.* One sentence of Grice in the counter-reading ("knows (and knows that I know that he knows)") does real work but arrives unintroduced; a reader who has not done Seminar 6 will not know why a theory of implicature settles anything. Either give Grice three sentences that say what the presumption is and why it matters here, or cut him. (§ "The counter-reading," final sentences.)
2. *The paper's strongest move is undersold.* The three-charges reading of 275d–e is the paper, and the draft states it well; but the collision section runs the charges past the agent in the order (i), (ii), (iii) and says of the third only that the defence is "another continuation." That is the charge that connects to Shanahan's "doesn't act *for itself*," and it deserves the longest paragraph, not the shortest. (§ "The agent, charge by charge," third paragraph.)
3. *Documentation.* The paper cites the course's own translation project as a source, which is right for this course, but it should say in a sentence what that project is (a model-extracted, code-checked table) so the reader can weigh it. (§ "What Thamus objects to," first paragraph; Works Cited.)

**Keep.** The opening inside 275d, which lands the claim at the end of the second paragraph exactly as asked. The close on "the attitude of life," which returns to the opening passage with a difference.

**Rewrite of the weakest paragraph** (the third charge): see synthesis.

## Judge 2 — The philologist

**Scores.** Claim 4 · Crux read 4 · AI from the text 5 · AI paper engaged 4 · Counter-reading 4 · Prose 4 · Format 2.

**Three problems.**
1. *An overclaim about the translations.* "a word the course's translation project follows through seventeen versions of this passage without finding two translators who agree on it." False. The project's README reports that the medical sense dominates the older versions (Ficino's *remedium*, Taylor's, Cary's and Wright's *medicine*, Cousin's *remède*) and that the German, Russian, and Finnish choose a neutral "means." Several translators agree. Say what the project found, or say less. (§ "What Thamus objects to," first paragraph.)
2. *A misplaced quotation.* "when Phaedrus admires it he disclaims it: 'I have been filled through the ears, like a pitcher'" (235c). The pitcher line comes *before* Socrates's first speech, when he says he could make a speech as good as Lysias's and different; it is not his response to Phaedrus's admiration afterward. The disclaimer is in advance. Fix the sequence. (§ "The attitude of life," first paragraph.)
3. *Stephanus numbers without a source.* Jowett's Gutenberg text carries no Stephanus numbers; the paper cites 275d, 276a, 271c–272b, 257b, 235c as if it did. The Theuth passage (274c–275b) was checked against Fowler's milestones in the course's Phaedrus-walk project; the others were not. Works Cited must say so, and "Fowler, 275a–b" in running text should become a parenthetical citation like the rest. Also: "the oak at Dodona gave true answers" is a stretch; Jowett's Socrates says the oaks "first gave prophetic utterances" and that the men of old would hear truth "even from 'oak or rock.'" Say what the text says.

**Keep.** The handling of Jowett's "specific" against Fowler's "elixir," which is exactly the kind of thing a translator's choice does to an argument. The careful "I do not want to make this more than it is" at the end of the collision, which keeps Shanahan's §8 limit in view.

## Judge 3 — The stylist

**Scores.** Prose 4 · Ends-rather-than-stops 5.

**Three problems.**
1. "Twenty-four centuries earlier, with one difference: the orphan has learned to talk." The number is throat-clearing; the sentence after the colon is the one that earns its place. Cut the century count or make it exact and quiet. (§ "The agent, charge by charge," third paragraph.)
2. The counter-reading's answer stacks three "It has … It has … It is" sentences and then a fourth "It cannot refrain. It can produce a refusal, but a refusal is a turn." The last two are the point; the run-up can lose one. (§ "The counter-reading," second paragraph.)
3. "and it would be a cheap reading to stop there. The harder reading is the one 275d already contains." Two sentences announcing a reading before giving it. Give it.

**Keep.** "The agent has removed the silence, and left the attitude." The paragraph beginning "So the dialogue says two things."

## Synthesis

Where the judges disagree: the philologist wants the Stephanus apparatus visible; the stylist would keep it out of the prose. Both: the numbers stay in parentheses, and the apparatus goes in one sentence of the Works Cited entry.

Changes made in `W08_draft_v2.md`:
1. Translation overclaim corrected to what the *pharmakon* project found (medical sense in the older versions; "means" in the German, Russian, Finnish; Jowett's own change from 1871 to 1892).
2. The pitcher quotation re-sequenced: Socrates disclaims the speech before giving it.
3. Dodona sentence corrected to the text.
4. The third-charge paragraph expanded; it now carries Shanahan's "doesn't act *for itself*" and "no-one at home" as the paper's central equation, with the century count cut.
5. Grice given three sentences that say what the hearer's presumption is, so the final sentence of the counter-reading has something to stand on.
6. One sentence added describing the *pharmakon* project's method, and the Works Cited entry for Jowett now states the Stephanus situation; "Fowler, 275a–b" moved into parentheses.
7. The stylist's cuts: "cheap reading … harder reading" replaced by the reading itself; the "It has … It is" run trimmed by one.

Not accepted: none of the judges proposed a change to the claim, and none was made.
