# W05 — Outline

Budget: 2,500–3,000 words (brief, assumed). Paragraph-level. The paragraph the whole paper depends on is marked ★.

## 1. Opening on a passage (~300 words)

- **Claim of the section.** Socrates's objection to writing is that it cannot be questioned; the dialogue agent can be questioned endlessly and that is its defect.
- **Sources.** 275d–e (Jowett), quoted: "the creations of the painter have the attitude of life … the speaker always gives one unvarying answer." Shanahan §5, quoted: regenerate and it "will sometimes name an entirely different object."
- **Move.** Begin inside 275d: the painted figure, the question, the silence. Then the modern scene: ask again, get another answer. The claim arrives last: not refuted, inverted; same cause.

## 2. The reading (~1,000 words)

- **2a. Thamus's complaint is about location, not truth** (~350). 274e–275b. Theuth's claim ("a specific both for the memory and for the wit"; Fowler "an elixir of memory and wisdom"); Thamus's reply dismantles it clause by clause: "trust to the external written characters," "an aid not to memory, but to reminiscence" (Fowler "of reminding"), "hearers of many things and will have learned nothing," "appear to be omniscient and will generally know nothing." Note the key word via the *pharmakon* project: Jowett "specific," Fowler "elixir," the Greek *pharmakon* that is remedy and drug at once. Point: nothing here says the letters lie. The complaint is that knowing has moved outside the knower.
- **2b. The three parts of the objection** ★ (~350). 275d–e read as three sentences with three charges: (i) questioned, it "always gives one unvarying answer" (it cannot be examined); (ii) "tumbled about anywhere … know not to whom they should reply, to whom not" (it cannot choose its hearer); (iii) "no parent to protect them; and they cannot protect or defend themselves" (it is undefended). Only (i) is about sameness. (ii) and (iii) are about the absence of a speaker. Then 276a: the living word's three marks answer the three charges in order: "can defend itself" (iii), "knows when to speak and when to be silent" (i and ii). Silence is the mark that answers the unvarying answer: a living word can decline.
- **2c. The brat and the pitcher** (~300). 257b, Lysias "the father of the brat"; 235c, Socrates "filled through the ears, like a pitcher … forgotten … who was my informant." The dialogue is already full of words with doubtful parents, including Socrates's own. The objection to writing is made by a speaker who disowns his speeches. Keep this short; it sets up the counter-reading and the close.

## 3. The collision (~700 words)

- **3a. The agent in Shanahan's description** (~250). §2: an LLM "embedded in a turn-taking system" with a prompt "in the style of a script or play"; §4: "a performer in improvisational theatre"; §6: "role-play all the way down," "doesn't act for itself"; §8: "no-one at home."
- **3b. The inversion** (~250). §5 quoted at length: the 20-questions game; "will not … commit to it"; regenerate and get "an entirely different object." Set against 275d: the written word's answer never changes; the agent's never holds. Then run the three charges: (i) the agent can be examined and never stands examination, since each answer is a fresh continuation; (ii) it replies to anyone, which is 275d's "tumbled about anywhere" made into a product; (iii) it defends its answers, but the defence is role-played (§6), which is to say undefended in Socrates's sense. The cause is the one Socrates gave: no parent. Shanahan's phrase for it is "no-one at home."
- **3c. The appearance of wisdom** (~200). Fowler's Thamus: "they will read many things without instruction and will therefore seem to know many things." The agent is the reader who has read everything without instruction. Thamus's "show of wisdom without the reality" describes a system trained to continue text. Do not overclaim: Shanahan §8 says fine-tuning may change the picture; the paper's claim is about the base model's description, which is what the course assigned.

## 4. The counter-reading (~450 words)

- **4a. Stated at full strength** (~200). The agent is the written word repaired. It answers back; it adapts: §3, the role "extended and/or overwritten" by the conversation. And adaptation is exactly what Socrates asks of the true orator at 271c–272b, who knows "the differences of human souls" and "when he should speak and when he should refrain." Jowett's introduction supports the objection from the other side: speech and writing "have really different functions," and writing's is to speak "to all the world"; an agent is speech's elasticity with writing's reach, the best of both.
- **4b. Answer** (~250). 271c–272b asks for two things, not one: knowledge of souls *and* knowledge of "when he should refrain." The agent has the first in a degraded form (it conditions on the conversation) and the second not at all (§2: the turn system; it must take its turn). Adaptation without the capacity to refrain is not the living word; it is the written word that has learned to vary. The 20-questions test (§5) shows the difference: the orator who knows souls has something to say; the agent has only what is consistent with what was said. Grice p. 50 in one sentence: the questioner supplies the presumption that someone "knows (and knows that I know that he knows)"; with the agent, that supply is the whole of the knowing. Concede what the counter-reading wins: Socrates's charge (ii), that writing cannot choose its hearer, is partly met by a system that conditions on its hearer. The paper's claim stands on (i) and (iii).

## 5. Close (~250 words)

- Return to 275d: "the attitude of life." Then 275b–c: Phaedrus, "you can easily invent tales of Egypt," and Socrates, "you seem to consider not whether a thing is or is not true, but who the speaker is." The dialogue tells us not to ask who is speaking, and its whole objection to writing is that no one is. The agent makes the two instructions collide: we are told not to care who speaks, and we cannot find out. End on the garden of Adonis (276b–c): seeds that come up in eight days for show. Last sentence on the text: what the written word could not do, and the agent does too well.
