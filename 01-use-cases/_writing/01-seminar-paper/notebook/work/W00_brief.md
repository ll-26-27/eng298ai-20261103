- **Deliverable.** A seminar paper that reads a course text in relation to
  the course's questions about AI, written with AI used "in specific,
  intentional ways," and documenting that use. **Confirm:** length (assume
  2,500 to 3,500 words), format (assume a PDF or HTML page on Canvas), due

- **What the unit asks.** "How to use AI as a tool for writing. This

  point is not to outsource writing to AI. The point is to use AI in

## Audience voice to write for

A graduate seminar paper for a comparatist. A claim about a text, made by
reading it, with the passage on the page and located so it can be checked.
The AI question has to arise from the text, not be laid over it. The paper
should say, in its own voice, where the model worked and where the writer
did, because the course treats that as part of the thinking.

## Rubric we will judge against (inferred; confirm with the instructor)

1. Does the paper make a claim about a text, or announce a topic?
2. Is the crux passage quoted, located (Stephanus number, chapter, page), and read, not summarized?
3. Does the AI question arise from the text?
4. Does the paper engage at least one of the AI papers, for something it actually says?
5. Is there a counter-reading, stated at its strongest, and answered?
6. Is every use of AI documented, explained, and reflected upon, in the paper itself?
7. Prose: exact, light, no throat-clearing; it ends rather than stops.
8. Length, format, citations consistent.

## Topic space (where the angles will come from)

- Part I, prehistory: Shelley, Wilde (the novel and "The Critic as Artist"), Čapek, Bergson.
- Part II, dialogue: Plato's *Phaedrus* (especially 257c onward, the critique of writing), the *Analects*, Sacks–Schegloff–Jefferson on turn-taking, Grice on conversational implicature; Askell et al. on the fabricated assistant; Shanahan et al. on role-play.
- The twenty-two papers as the technical record the literary texts can be checked against.
