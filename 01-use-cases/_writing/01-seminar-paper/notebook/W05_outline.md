# W05 — Outline

**Runner:** the model. **Reads:** `work/W03_thesis.md`, `work/W04_sources.md`, `work/W00_brief.md` (for length). **Writes:** `work/W05_outline.md`.

## Shape

A literary-critical essay, not a report. Proportions for a paper of the
brief's length:

1. **Opening on a passage** (~10%). Begin inside the text: the crux passage
   or a smaller one that leads to it. No preamble about AI being everywhere.
   The claim arrives at the end of the opening, as the thing the passage has
   made it possible to say.
2. **The reading** (~35%). What the passage does, shown by reading it: the
   words, the turn, what it withholds. This is where the paper earns its
   claim. Two or three movements, each anchored in a quoted sentence.
3. **The collision** (~25%). The course question enters: the AI paper, the
   second text, the lens. Not a comparison for its own sake; the second text
   has to change what the first one means, or sharpen it.
4. **The counter-reading** (~15%). The strongest objection, stated fairly,
   with its passage, and answered. If it cannot be fully answered, say what
   it costs.
5. **Close** (~10%). Return to the opening passage with the difference the
   paper has made to it. End; do not summarize.
6. **Works cited.**

## Outline format

For each section: the claim it makes (one sentence), the sources it uses
(from the bank, by tag), the quotations to use verbatim (at most one per
paragraph), and a target word count. Paragraph-level, not sentence-level.
Mark the one paragraph the whole paper depends on.
