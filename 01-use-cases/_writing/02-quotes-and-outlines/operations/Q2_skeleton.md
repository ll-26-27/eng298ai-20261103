# Q2 — The text's skeleton

**Runner:** the model. **Reads:** the same text, and `work/Q1_quotes_<slug>.md`. **Writes:** `work/Q2_skeleton_<slug>.md`.

A reverse outline: not what the text is about, but what it does, step by
step, in the order it does it, with where each step happens. The test of a
good skeleton is that someone who has the text open can follow it with a
finger.

## Format

Numbered sections with page ranges, each with:

- **Move.** What the text does here, in one sentence with a verb: sets up a
  dispute, introduces a term, states a principle, lists, ranks, asks for a
  basis, concedes, gives examples, derives consequences.
- **Content.** Two to five lines of what it says, in the text's own terms.
- **Anchor.** The entry in the quote bank that belongs to this section, by
  page.

Then, at the end:

- **The spine.** The argument in five sentences, each pointing to a section.
- **The hinges.** Two or three places where the text could have gone another
  way and says so (an objection raised, a question deferred, a scheme the
  author calls too narrow). These are where a paper gets in.
- **What is not here.** What a reader might expect and the text does not do.
