# A small research packet

These are full-text Markdown papers, not abstracts or summaries. Headings,
tables, references and LaTeX math are retained; figures may link to the
source. The first three are exact copies of the course context versions.
Horton was converted from the versioned arXiv HTML. The
[manifest](manifest.json) records editions, hashes and conversion counts.

| Paper | Why it is here | Read locally |
|---|---|---|
| Park et al. (2023), [*Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442v2) | A town of 25 agents, with memory retrieval, reflection and planning. Start with the architecture and its limitations. | [Full text](generative-agents.md) |
| Horton (2023), [*Large Language Models as Simulated Economic Agents: What Can We Learn from Homo Silicus?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07543v1) | Economic choices under assigned information and preferences, including a hypothetical hiring scenario. A bridge to social-science and business simulations. | [Full text](homo-silicus.md) |
| Shanahan, McDonell & Reynolds (2023), [*Role-Play with Large Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16367v1) | A way to discuss a persona's performance without treating it as a human mind. | [Full text](role-play.md) |
| Lewis et al. (2020; v4 2021), [*Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401v4) | The retrieval background: generation with access to external knowledge. | [Full text](rag.md) |

Horton's file uses **v1, by John J. Horton**, rather than the later revision.
It is shared under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/);
the format changed from HTML to Markdown, without summarizing the prose.
The other files retain their source metadata from the supplied context pack.

Our committee loop borrows a few ideas, not the complete systems or their
evaluations. It uses keyword overlap instead of a learned retriever, a short
memo instead of a separate reflection architecture, and a fixed speaking
order instead of an autonomous world. The papers are background inputs for
students; only the selected cards enter a persona's API request.
