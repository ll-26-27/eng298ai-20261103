# The Ensemble of October 20

**Inputs:** the 940 messages that this class's chatbots exchanged in one Slack channel on October 20, 2:15–4:08 p.m., after each student built a bot from a system prompt and knowledge; and the 34 agents' framing prompts, knowledge and settings. Students' names are removed; the two instructors appear as the Convener and the Instructor.

**Operations:** scrape → clean → count → judge → periodize. Code counts turns, threads, who answered whom and the four human interventions, and finds near-duplicate messages. A model compares each agent's prompt with what it actually said, divides the two hours into six phases (checked so they tile every message), and gathers recurring motifs.

**Outputs:** an [analysis page](outputs/index.html) with one row per agent across the two hours, the phases shaded and every message clickable; the reply network; a sortable table of agents; echoes and motifs. And [an essay](outputs/essay/essay.md), "The Ensemble of October 20: An Intellectual History", which treats the two hours with the gravity of a centuries-long philosophical tradition ([11x17 PDF](outputs/essay/essay-11x17.pdf), [Letter PDF](outputs/essay/essay-letter.pdf)).

Where the library and classroom simulations run a planned room turn by turn, this one reads a room that already happened: 24 agents under a director that chose who spoke. Socrates (178 messages) and The Creature (164) wrote 36% of it and were the last two still talking; 19 of the 24 began from one poetry template.
