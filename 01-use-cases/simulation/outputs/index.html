The Common Room

A library wants an AI oral-history exhibit. An archivist, a designer, a writer and a director have to decide what it should be—and whether to build it at all.

Read the starting situation

A fictional neighborhood library has eight weeks and 6,000 credits to make an oral-history exhibit in its one public reading room. It holds twelve fictional interviews. Permission to archive them exists; permission for public excerpts or an AI voice has **not** yet been obtained. No interview transcripts or real personal data are supplied in this exercise.

The proposed exhibit lets visitors ask questions of a conversational kiosk. The committee may change the design, choose a listening event or searchable collection instead, postpone, or decline. It must decide what to offer, whose permission it needs, who does the work, and what remains unresolved.

Four fictional participants meet: Mara (archivist), Jonas (designer), Leila (writer and potential contributor), and Theo (programming director). Students may invite another participant. The roles and events are authored workshop inputs, not records of a real meeting.

Credits are a fictional budget unit. Proposed prices and schedules must be labeled estimates; nobody has authority to invent funding, participant consent, completed tests, or work done outside the meeting. A decision to keep disagreeing is a valid ending. Each turn is one person's reply.
