# Read against the dialogue

*Workshop editorial notes, written after the run. These are not additional
turns, model judgments, or part of any participant's input.*

The saved dialogue provides something to question, not a correct account
of learning. Three places to pause:

1. **Leonie, turns 2 → 7 → 17.** She begins with accurate unaided recall,
   concedes that this is not sufficient, and adds use in a later similar
   case. Has she explained why unaided recall remains necessary, or kept
   that part of her original position without answering Sam?
2. **Iris, turns 4 → 9 → 19.** She loosens verbal explanation into defending
   a step by prediction or repair. Does this answer Nico's objection, or
   make “giving a reason” so broad that any success could count? What would
   test the difference?
3. **The last round's claims about chatbots.** Nico says a delayed test would
   show “storage plus matching” (turn 18). What in this conversation licenses
   that mechanism claim? Iris and Sam appeal to exposure or stakes in the
   outcome (turns 19–20). Is having a stake necessary for a prediction to be
   testable, or is that a new criterion the class has not defended?

Try replacing one confident claim with a question. Or add a student whose
role is to ask what the group can actually infer from the evidence it has.
Keep these notes separate from the recorded dialogue; a polished page should
not hide the model's leaps or make the seminar seem more resolved than it is.
