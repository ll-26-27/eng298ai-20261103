Comparison of the French (Alcan, 1938 witness) with Brereton/Rothwell (1911). The date gap is noted, but this is not an edition collation.

| French phrase | Our wording | Brereton/Rothwell | Editorial trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| "un quiproquo de vaudeville" (S2) | "a vaudeville mix-up" | "an equivocal situation in a burlesque" | We keep the French genre name, which needs a note because "vaudeville" may suggest American variety shows. They use a different genre term, "burlesque," and a more abstract noun. This reads smoothly but moves away from the French labels. |
| "se dérobe sous l'effort, glisse, s'échappe, se redresse" (S4) | "gives way under the effort, slips off, escapes, springs back up" | "baffling every effort, of slipping away and escaping only to bob up again" | We keep four separate movements and the stacked rhythm, but the list is more mechanical. They fold the sequence into a flowing clause and add "a knack." That sounds natural but compresses the series, and "baffling" is not a literal match for "se dérobe." |
| "la fantaisie comique" (S5) | "the comic fantasy" | "the comic spirit" | Ours is close in form but may suggest a genre, so it needs a note. Theirs avoids that, but "spirit" is, in my interpretation, more abstract and less playful. It also shifts the emphasis toward an animating principle. |

Neither version is objectively superior. Each trades literal closeness against readability in a different way.
