## S1

What does laughter mean? What lies at the bottom of the laughable?

## S2

What would one find in common between a clown's grimace, a play on words, a vaudeville mix-up, and a scene of refined comedy?

*Note: "Vaudeville" here means the French genre of light stage comedy built on intrigue and misunderstanding, not American variety shows. "Pitre" is a stock low-comic performer, so "clown" is slightly narrower than the French suggests but is the most familiar English equivalent.*

## S3

What distillation will give us the essence, always the same, from which so many different products borrow either their intrusive odor or their delicate perfume?

## S4

The greatest thinkers, from Aristotle on, have taken on this small problem, which always gives way under the effort, slips off, escapes, springs back up, an impertinent challenge flung at philosophical speculation.

## S5

Our excuse for approaching the problem in our turn is that we will not aim to confine the comic fantasy within a definition. We see in it, above all, something living.

*Note: "Fantaisie comique" means the playful, imaginative side of the comic, treated as a living phenomenon rather than a literary genre. "Fantasy" is a short but imperfect rendering. The trade-off is brevity against the risk that readers take it as a genre label.*

## S6

We will treat it, however light it may be, with the respect owed to life. We will limit ourselves to watching it grow and blossom.
