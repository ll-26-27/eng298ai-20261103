| Seg. | French | Draft | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S4 | "se dérobe sous l'effort" | "dodges the effort" | **Mistranslation (minor).** "Dodges the effort" suggests the problem avoids the work. The French says it gives way *under* the effort, as the ground does under a foot. This also blurs the sequence of evasions. | "always gives way under the effort, slips away, escapes, springs back up" |
| S2 | "Que trouverait-on de commun" | "What could… possibly have in common?" | **Shift, not an outright error.** The impersonal, conditional *on* (a search by an inquirer) becomes a question about what the items "could" share. "Possibly" is an addition. The question is still real. | Optionally: "What would one find in common between…?" Otherwise keep the draft and drop "possibly." |
| S5 | "la fantaisie comique" | "the comic fantasy" | **Interpretive risk.** "Fantasy" may read as a genre, but the French is the playful, imaginative comic phenomenon. The brief asks for a note rather than a paraphrase. | Keep the short rendering and add a note. Consider "comic fancy" if the genre reading is too strong. |
| S3 | "ou leur indiscrète odeur ou leur parfum délicat" | "either their intrusive odor or their delicate perfume" | **Strength.** The "either…or" structure, the distillation image, and the odor/perfume contrast are preserved. "Intrusive" is an interpretive choice for *indiscrète*, but a defensible one. | Retain. |

Other segments (S1, S6, and the "impertinent challenge" in S4) track the French closely, so I have not listed them.
