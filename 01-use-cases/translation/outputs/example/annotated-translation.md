# Translating Bergson

*Le Rire*, Chapter I · French → English

A worked AI translation for discussion. Segment labels and notes are editorial.

| Segment | French source | New English translation |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Que signifie le rire ? Qu’y a-t-il au fond du risible ? | What does laughter mean? What lies at the bottom of the laughable? |
| S2 | Que trouverait-on de commun entre une grimace de pitre, un jeu de mots, un quiproquo de vaudeville, une scène de fine comédie ? | What would one find in common between a clown's grimace, a play on words, a vaudeville mix-up, and a scene of refined comedy? [1] |
| S3 | Quelle distillation nous donnera l’essence, toujours la même, à laquelle tant de produits divers empruntent ou leur indiscrète odeur ou leur parfum délicat ? | What distillation will give us the essence, always the same, from which so many different products borrow either their intrusive odor or their delicate perfume? [2] |
| S4 | Les plus grands penseurs, depuis Aristote, se sont attaqués à ce petit problème, qui toujours se dérobe sous l’effort, glisse, s’échappe, se redresse, impertinent défi jeté à la spéculation philosophique. | The greatest thinkers, from Aristotle on, have taken on this small problem, which always gives way under the effort, slips off, escapes, springs back up, an impertinent challenge flung at philosophical speculation. [3] |
| S5 | Notre excuse, pour aborder le problème à notre tour, est que nous ne viserons pas à enfermer la fantaisie comique dans une définition. Nous voyons en elle, avant tout, quelque chose de vivant. | Our excuse for approaching the problem in our turn is that we will not aim to confine the comic fantasy within a definition. We see in it, above all, something living. [4] |
| S6 | Nous la traiterons, si légère soit-elle, avec le respect qu’on doit à la vie. Nous nous bornerons à la regarder grandir et s’épanouir. | We will treat it, however light it may be, with the respect owed to life. We will limit ourselves to watching it grow and blossom. |

## Four choices to inspect

1. **S2: quiproquo de vaudeville → a vaudeville mix-up.** Vaudeville here is the French genre of light stage comedy built on intrigue; keeping it preserves the cultural label. A plainer alternative, 'farce's mistaken-identity gag,' clarifies for Americans but loses the genre name.

2. **S3: Quelle distillation nous donnera l’essence → What distillation will give us the essence.** Keeping 'distillation' literally preserves the image of one constant essence yielding many scents; 'intrusive' renders indiscrète as a smell lacking manners. Alternative: 'What process of extraction' would be plainer but lose the chemistry.

3. **S4: se dérobe sous l’effort, glisse, s’échappe, se redresse → gives way under the effort, slips off, escapes, springs back up.** Four unconnected verbs keep the sequence of evasions; 'gives way under' suggests ground yielding. 'Springs back up' approximates se redresse. Alternative: 'eludes, slides, escapes, rights itself' sounds more philosophical but less vivid.

4. **S5: enfermer la fantaisie comique dans une définition → confine the comic fantasy within a definition.** Fantaisie comique means playful comic imagination, set against definition and 'something living.' 'Fantasy' may suggest a genre; 'comic fancy' or 'whimsy' would stress play, though whimsy risks sounding trivial.

Which choice would you change—and what would your version gain or lose?
