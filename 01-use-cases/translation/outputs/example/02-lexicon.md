| Seg. | French | Contextual sense (my interpretation) | English options | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S2 | grimace de pitre | A stock low-comic performer's face-pulling | a clown's grimace / a buffoon's grimace | "Clown" is familiar but suggests the circus. "Buffoon" is closer to a stage fool but sounds dated. |
| S2 | quiproquo de vaudeville | A mix-up where one person or thing is taken for another, in light stage comedy | a vaudeville mix-up / a farce's mistaken-identity gag | "Vaudeville" may evoke American variety shows, so it needs a note. "Farce" is clearer but drops the French genre name. |
| S3 | indiscrète odeur | A smell that obtrudes, set against "parfum délicat" (the socially tinged contrast is my reading) | intrusive odor / pungent smell | "Intrusive" keeps the manners-tinged personification. "Pungent" is more natural but reads as strength alone. |
| S4 | se dérobe… glisse, s'échappe, se redresse | A sequence of evasions: gives way, slides, escapes, pops back upright | dodges, slips, escapes, springs back / eludes, slides, escapes, rights itself | The first set keeps four distinct, vivid movements. The second sounds more abstract and philosophical. "Se redresse" is the hardest to render. |
| S5 | fantaisie comique | The playful, imaginative comic phenomenon as a living thing, not a defined genre | comic fantasy / comic whimsy | "Fantasy" suggests a genre or a specific kind of imagining. "Whimsy" gets the lightness but may trivialize. Either needs a note. |
| S6 | grandir et s'épanouir | Natural growth ending in full flowering, with the author as observer | grow and blossom / grow and flourish | "Blossom" keeps the botanical image. "Flourish" is more idiomatic but less concrete. |
