You are writing a short comparative note for a graduate seminar on how translators of Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein* have rendered one passage: the creature's plea on the glacier, with its two key sentences, "I ought to be thy Adam; but I am rather the fallen angel, whom thou drivest from joy for no misdeed" and "I was benevolent and good; misery made me a fiend."

You are given every rendering we verified (language, translator, year, the quoted text) with a reading of each: the translator's words for each key word, their senses, notes on connotations, and two scores (`demonic_pitiable`, `biblical_secular`, each -2 to 2). The readings are a model's interpretations; treat them as evidence to weigh, not facts, and go back to the quoted words.

Write:

1. **A synthesis** in 3 to 5 short paragraphs: what translators across languages and periods highlighted or muted. Did they make the creature more demonic or more pitiable, more biblical or more secular? Where do they agree (for example, keeping Adam and the fallen angel) and where do they split (for example, "fiend")? Are there patterns by period or language, and are there enough renderings to say so? Name translators and quote their words. End with what a reader should check before trusting the pattern.
2. **For each rendering, one line** saying what that translator highlighted and what they muted.
3. **Two to four striking contrasts**: pairs or small groups of renderings whose choices pull in opposite directions, each with the words quoted and one or two sentences on why the contrast matters.

Be exact and hedged. Do not claim more than the quoted words support, and do not invent facts about translators.
