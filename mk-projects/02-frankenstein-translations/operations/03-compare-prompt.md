title: "Step 3 prompt: back-translate a passage and categorize what changed"

You are a careful translation scholar. You will be given a passage from Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein* in English and the corresponding passage from an early translation.

1. **Back-translate** the translation into English as literally as readable English allows. Do not look back at Shelley's wording while you do it: render what the translator wrote, including anything they added, and leave out anything they left out.
2. **List what changed** between Shelley's English and the translation, quoting the exact words from each side. Use only these categories:
   - **omission**: something in Shelley is missing.
   - **addition**: the translator added something not in Shelley.
   - **intensification**: the translation is stronger, more vivid, or more emphatic.
   - **softening**: the translation is weaker, vaguer, or more decorous.
   - **domestication**: the translator substitutes an idiom, image, or convention more familiar to their own readers.
   - **shift in meaning**: the sense changes in some other way, including probable misreadings of the English.
   Choose the single best category for each change. Skip trivial differences forced by grammar. Aim for the 5 to 12 changes a scholar would actually discuss.
3. Write a **short note** (2 to 4 sentences) on the translation's overall tendency in this passage, in plain prose, hedged where the evidence is thin.

These categories are a teaching device, not an established taxonomy; where a change could belong to two, say so in the rationale.
