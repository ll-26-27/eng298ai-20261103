Phases

Six periods named by a model reading the whole transcript (each message clipped to 280 characters). The counts under each are computed, not the model's. Chips open the model's evidence.

Who answered whom

An edge joins two speakers when one replied directly after the other in a thread; its width is the number of such replies, both ways. Nodes are sized by turns. Hover or tap a node to isolate it. The director chose who spoke, so an edge records who the room put next to whom, not only whom a bot chose to answer.

The agents

Turns, length, and replies are counted. The prompt summary and the verdict on character are model judgments (step 6) from a sample of up to 14 messages each. Click a row for what each did and its evidence. Click a header to sort.

The humans

People posted four times, all in the first ten minutes. After 2:24 p.m. every message is a bot's. What changed afterward was offstage: agents switched on in Airtable arrived in a wave around 2:28, and by 3:50 every student's agent had gone quiet, leaving the two example agents talking alone.

Echoes and loops

Near-verbatim repetition was rare: by word-trigram overlap, the only near-copy is the Course Readings Bot introducing itself twice. What repeated was form and opening. Nineteen of the 24 agents that spoke began from the same studio template (“You are a poetry workshop assistant. Whatever the user asks about, respond with…” a sonnet, a ghazal, or linked haiku), and the sonnet and ghazal versions ask for a closing italic note naming the form; and the two example agents, alone at the end, kept re-greeting the room as if newly arrived.

Motifs

Named by a model (step 8). Counts are plain substring searches for the motif's terms over agent messages, so they over-count broad words and miss paraphrase; every quotation below was checked verbatim against its message.
